title: श्रीदुर्गासप्तशती | Shri Durga Saptashati | The seven hundred verses of the Goddess (Devi Mahatmya, Chandi Path)
author: From the Markandeya Purana, as told by the sage Markandeya | traditional
occasions: navratri, dussehra
when: Read in full on each day of Navratri, or spread across the nine days, and on Ashtami and Navami with a havan. Many homes read the three stutis (chapters 4, 5 and 11) even when there is no time for the whole path.
notes: 700 mantras in 13 chapters, chapters 81 to 93 of the Markandeya Purana. They form three episodes: the first (chapter 1, Mahakali), the middle (chapters 2 to 4, Mahalakshmi) and the last (chapters 5 to 13, Mahasaraswati). Verse numbers follow the Gita Press edition, which gives speaker lines such as ऋषिरुवाच a number of their own. All 13 chapters are entered in full, each with its dhyana. The Sanskrit has been checked verse by verse against one online edition that follows Gita Press numbering, but not yet proofread against a printed copy; bija syllables such as ॐ before each verse are left out. If you spot a mistake, please use the correction link.

chapter: 1 | मधुकैटभवध | Madhu Kaitabh Vadh | The slaying of Madhu and Kaitabh | 104 | King Suratha, driven from his kingdom, and the merchant Samadhi, cast out by his family, meet in the forest hermitage of the sage Medha and ask why their minds still cling to what betrayed them. The sage tells them of Mahamaya, the Goddess’s power of illusion. At the end of an age, as Vishnu sleeps on the cosmic waters, two demons, Madhu and Kaitabh, spring from the wax of his ears and attack Brahma. Brahma praises the Goddess as Yoganidra, the sleep of Vishnu, and she withdraws from Vishnu, who wakes and slays the demons.
chapter: 2 | महिषासुरसैन्यवध | Mahishasur Sainya Vadh | The destruction of Mahishasur’s armies | 69 | Mahishasur, the buffalo demon, has defeated the gods and taken heaven. Their anger pours out of Brahma, Vishnu, Shiv and every god as light, and the light gathers into the form of the Goddess. Each god gives her a weapon: Shiv his trident, Vishnu his discus, Indra his thunderbolt, the Himalaya a lion to ride. She roars, and in the battle that follows she destroys Mahishasur’s generals and their armies.
chapter: 3 | महिषासुरवध | Mahishasur Vadh | The slaying of Mahishasur | 44 | The Goddess kills the generals Chikshura, Chamara and others. Mahishasur himself attacks, changing from buffalo to lion to man to elephant and back. She drinks the divine drink, laughs, presses him down with her foot and pierces him with her trident, and as he half emerges from the buffalo’s mouth she strikes off his head.
chapter: 4 | शक्रादिस्तुति | Shakradi Stuti | Indra and the gods praise the Goddess | 42 | With Mahishasur dead, Indra and the gods bow before the Goddess and praise her in one of the great hymns of the text: she is the power behind all the gods, the fortune in good homes and the misfortune in wicked ones, and her anger itself is a mercy. She promises to come whenever they remember her in distress.
chapter: 5 | देवीदूतसंवाद | Devi Doot Samvad | The gods’ hymn, and the demons’ messenger | 129 | Shumbh and Nishumbh have seized the three worlds. The gods go to the Himalaya and praise the Goddess as the one who dwells in every being, in the hymn that begins या देवी सर्वभूतेषु. Parvati comes to bathe in the Ganga, and from her body comes forth Ambika. Chand and Mund see her beauty and tell Shumbh, who sends the messenger Sugriva to ask for her hand. She answers that long ago she vowed to marry only the one who defeats her in battle.
chapter: 6 | धूम्रलोचनवध | Dhumralochan Vadh | The slaying of Dhumralochan | 24 | Shumbh, enraged by her answer, sends his general Dhumralochan with sixty thousand demons to drag the Goddess to him by her hair. With a single sound, “हुं”, she turns him to ash, and her lion destroys his army.
chapter: 7 | चण्डमुण्डवध | Chand Mund Vadh | The slaying of Chand and Mund | 27 | Chand and Mund come with a great army. The Goddess’s face darkens with anger and from her brow springs Kali, gaunt and terrible, wearing a garland of skulls, who devours the army and beheads both demons. She brings their heads to the Goddess, who names her Chamunda.
chapter: 8 | रक्तबीजवध | Raktabeej Vadh | The slaying of Raktabeej | 63 | The powers of the gods take form as the Matrikas (Brahmani, Maheshwari, Kaumari, Vaishnavi, Varahi, Narasimhi and Aindri) and join the battle, and the Goddess sends Shiv himself as her messenger, so she is called Shivaduti. Every drop of Raktabeej’s blood that falls becomes a new demon, until Kali drinks the blood before it can touch the ground and the Goddess slays him.
chapter: 9 | निशुम्भवध | Nishumbh Vadh | The slaying of Nishumbh | 41 | Nishumbh charges the Goddess. She fells him; he rises with ten thousand arms; when she pierces his heart, another being emerges from it crying “Stop!”, and she laughs and cuts off its head. The Matrikas destroy the rest of his army.
chapter: 10 | शुम्भवध | Shumbh Vadh | The slaying of Shumbh | 32 | Shumbh taunts the Goddess for relying on others’ strength. She answers that she is alone in the world, and all the Matrikas merge back into her. They fight with weapons, then hand to hand in the sky, until she hurls him to the earth and pierces his heart with her spear.
chapter: 11 | नारायणीस्तुति | Narayani Stuti | The gods’ hymn to Narayani | 55 | After Shumbh is slain, the gods led by Agni praise the Goddess as Narayani, in verses that are recited on their own every day, among them सर्वमङ्गलमङ्गल्ये. She grants them a boon: whenever demons trouble the world she will come again, and she foretells the forms she will take, as Vindhyavasini, Raktadantika, Shatakshi, Shakambhari, Durga, Bhima and Bhramari.
chapter: 12 | फलस्तुति | Phalastuti | The blessings of reciting this text | 41 | The Goddess tells the gods what reciting her story brings: protection from enemies, fire, water, illness and bad dreams, peace in the home and the growth of the family. Reading it on the eighth, ninth and fourteenth days of the fortnight, and in the great autumn worship each year, pleases her especially. She then vanishes, and the sage sums up how she protects the world.
chapter: 13 | सुरथवैश्ययोर्वरप्रदान | Surath Vaishya Var Pradan | Boons for King Suratha and the merchant | 29 | Hearing the story, Suratha and Samadhi go to a riverbank and worship the Goddess for three years with images of clay. She appears. Suratha asks for his kingdom back in this life and a kingdom that cannot be lost in the next, and is promised that he will be reborn as Savarni, the eighth Manu. The merchant, now free of attachment, asks for the knowledge that ends all clinging, and is granted it.
